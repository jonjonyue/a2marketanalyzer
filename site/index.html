How this is calculated

  • Cost per craft comes from aion2.exchange's crafting model: expected value over many crafts at mastery 100, with each material priced at the cheapest of buying it, converting Odyle, or crafting it from cheaper inputs. "Buy everything" uses the site's all-market cost instead.
  • Sell price: "Conservative" takes the lower of the cheapest current listing and the average sale price, so a single overpriced listing can't inflate the margin. The average sale price is the price of each listing just before it sold, over the last 7 days.
  • Profit per craft = sell price × (1 − sales tax − listing fee) − cost. Defaults are the exchange's 10% sales tax and ~2% listing fee. This assumes the item sells on its first listing; every relist costs another listing fee.
  • Your crafting proficiency sets each craft's success rate, per skill (Novice 1–50, then Professional 1–50; default Professional 25). With ΔL = your level − the recipe's level: at ΔL 0 a craft succeeds about 74%, rising on a log curve (biggest gains in the first ~10 levels) to the recipe's cap at ΔL 45+. Caps for the Splendent steps come from aion2.exchange (93.1%, 94.6%, 96.8%); True Dragon's cap is assumed to be 96.8%. Lower success means more attempts, so higher cost and capital. Below a recipe's level you can't craft it ("Needs …"). Your levels are remembered in this browser, so they're set the next time you open the page (a shared link with its own levels shows those instead, without changing yours). This is an approximate community formula, not the game's exact table.
  • Capital needed is the barrier to entry: the kinah to have ready for a 90% chance of making one. Splendent items only come from a combo roll, so the average cost hides bad luck: an Artisan's Splendent averages about 4 attempts but needs 9 attempts' worth of materials for 90% odds. True Dragon needs one attempt. Return on capital = profit ÷ capital needed, so a 2M profit on 10M of capital (20%) ranks below cheaper crafts with better ratios. Margin is profit ÷ the average cost.
  • Sold per day is estimated from hourly listing snapshots, because the game publishes no sales statistics for NA servers. Over the last 7 days, listings that disappear count as sales, except those that vanish 72 hours after appearing (expired). A snapshot where the count holds but the cheapest price jumps counts as one sale (the cheapest sold and was replaced). Hourly snapshots miss items listed and bought within the same hour, so the count is scaled up by a conservative 1.2× (on an EU server with official stats, the raw count caught about two-thirds of real sales, so this still leans low) and rounded to a tenth. Cancelled listings look like sales and can't be separated. Hover a number to see the raw count. Days of supply = current listings ÷ sold per day.
  • Expert's Splendent cost comes from aion2.exchange's model too: it's the second tier of each Artisan's Splendent chain (the Splendent and Expert's Splendent combo steps, with their success rates), and Artisan's Splendent consumes about four of them per item.
  • True Dragon (Elyos) and Star Dragon (Asmodian, the same recipes with different item IDs) share the True Dragon tier buttons. Cost (≈) is one craft attempt: the Artisan's Splendent item of the same slot (the cheaper of crafting it, per aion2.exchange's model, or buying it) plus the recipe's materials at the server's cheapest listings. Splendent True Dragon only comes from the 25% combo roll on that craft, so it costs 4× an attempt. Splendent True Dragon items can also be made by transfer crafting a True Dragon item, but the result is bound to the character and can't be sold, so that route doesn't set a seller's cost. It does set a ceiling: expand a Splendent True Dragon row to see what a buyer would pay to transfer one (a True Dragon item, cheaper of crafting or buying, plus the transfer materials, ÷ success). A ▲ next to the sell price means it's above that, so buyers can make their own for less. Recipes from questlog.gg. Hover a cost for the breakdown. Materials are priced at the cheapest single listing, so buying in bulk can cost more. Turnover / day = sell price after fees × sold per day. Slots never listed on the server aren't shown.
  • What to craft with your kinah: enter your kinah and the dashboard fills it with crafts worth making (Top pick or Craft it, within the filters you've set), best return on capital first. Each craft gets one, plus extras only if the market has room for them within one 3-day listing: the current listings and your first sell at the market's pace, and after that you only win your share of sales (other sellers keep relisting, and extra copies of your own just queue behind your first). That's 1 + your share × (sold per day × 3 − current listings − 1), rounded down. It sets aside enough for 90% odds of making them all, so bad luck on a combo craft doesn't leave you short. Capital needed is shown in orange where it's more than you have. List at depends on how crowded the market is. With 3 or more listed, just under the cheapest listing, but no higher than the typical recent sale price, so overpriced listings that aren't moving don't set your price. With 2 or fewer listed, it looks at sales over the past week that happened while the market was that thin: if at least 2 sold, it suggests their median price, since scarcity often lets higher prices sell (capped at 1.5× the typical price). Without that history, just under the cheapest listing, or the typical sale price if nothing is listed. Hover the price for the reasoning. The plan's profit uses that price. Your kinah is saved in this browser only and never goes into share links. Once set, every purchase, listing fee and sale you log in the Profit Calculator updates it.
  • Daily profit pool = profit per craft × sold per day; your daily scales that by the share of sales you expect to win. Crafts are ranked by the pool.
  • Verdicts, checked in this order: Loss (profit ≤ 0) · Oversupplied (more than 5 days of supply, i.e. listings ÷ sold per day; listings only last 3 days) · Slow seller (a listing you post now would wait more than 3 days to sell: (listings + 1) ÷ sold per day, so it would likely expire first) · Low return (under 15% return on capital: profitable, but a run of bad luck on the combo wipes it out) · Top pick (at least 50% return on capital, 1+ sold/day and at most 3 days of supply) · Craft it (everything else that's profitable and selling). Needs …, Cost TBD, No sales data and No price mean the item can't be judged yet.
